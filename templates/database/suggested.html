Suggested recent literature

Articles found by an AI-assisted PubMed search that may be missing from {{ site_name }}, shared so that curators and users can see what is being considered.

These articles are not part of the database yet. Each one is a suggestion. The AI judged it from the title and abstract only and can be wrong. A curator reads the full paper, and only then is it added to {{ site_name }}. A column on this page shows the curators' decision as soon as there is one.

How the list is made

  1. Search. For every year from 2021 to 2026, PubMed is searched for primary research on cancer drugs together with immune terms, and the 100 best matches of each year are taken. Every PubMed ID comes from PubMed itself, never from the AI.
  2. AI screening. Google Gemini reads each title and abstract and says whether the study tests a named anticancer drug and measures its effect on the immune system, the same scope as the database. In a first check, it marked 33 of 40 articles that curators had already accepted as likely relevant (and 1 as uncertain), and it rejected all 20 unrelated cancer articles.
  3. Curator check. Curators read each suggested paper in full and decide. Accepted papers are entered into the database with their doses, techniques and immune terms.

Why this exists: the database holds only about 10 articles a year for 2021–2024, and a plain PubMed search cannot judge whether a paper fits the scope.
